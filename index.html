What the check does and does not know

Every height in this app is above the takeoff point, obstacle heights included — so a box on a slope is only as right as the height you gave it. The check measures the planned path: the legs between waypoints, including the long ones between one pass and the next. It knows nothing about the climb out from home, the return leg, wind push, or GNSS error beyond whatever you put in the clearance.

Obstacles also block the camera: the coverage score stops counting a surface it can only see through a box. They are never scored themselves — a tree next to the house is not a surface you failed to photograph.

Walk the site

Survey on foot. Stop next to a thing, say how tall it is, tap Here. One stop is one obstacle, and its box is grown to match the accuracy your phone reports — so it encloses the thing wherever inside that circle you actually stood. When you are done, the area you walked becomes the area to capture, and the lowest orbit ring is set just over the tallest thing you found.

Stand next to the first thing and tap Here.

What a walk can and cannot tell you

A phone fix is metres, not centimetres, and it is worst under exactly the canopy that makes walking worth doing. A stop is refused past ±25 m, and anything looser than ±8 m says so — but a box that encloses the tree is still only a box, and the height is whatever you judged standing under it.

Every height here is above your takeoff point, not above the ground you are standing on. On a slope those are different numbers, and nothing in this app knows the difference.

Getting the KMZ into DJI Fly

With the controller plugged into this machine, the panel above does all of this for you. By hand, on a phone, or from the published copy of this page:

  1. In DJI Fly, create any throwaway waypoint mission so the app makes a mission folder.
  2. Connect the RC / phone and open Android/data/dji.go.v5/files/waypoint/.
  3. Each mission is a folder named with a UUID containing one <same-UUID>.kmz.
  4. Rename the exported file to that UUID and overwrite the one inside the folder.
  5. Restart DJI Fly. The mission appears in the waypoint list.

On iOS the path is DJI Fly/wayline_mission/. DJI Fly caps a mission at 200 waypoints — longer plans are exported as numbered parts to fly back to back.
